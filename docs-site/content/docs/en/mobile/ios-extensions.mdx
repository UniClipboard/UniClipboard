This page is iOS-only. Besides its main screen, the UniClipboard iOS app ships two
system-level extensions — a **custom keyboard** and a **share extension** — that let
you read and write the clipboard and push content to your desktop without opening the
app. Android users can skip this page.

Prerequisite: you've already installed the app and configured at least one server via
[Connect to Desktop](./connect). For how to turn on phone sync on the desktop and how
to get pairing credentials, see [Mobile Sync](../core-features/mobile-sync) and
[Pairing and Sync](../guides/pairing).

## Keyboard Extension

The keyboard extension is a custom input method. Once installed, you can switch to the
**UniClip Keyboard** in any app's text field to type out your most recent clipboard
content directly, or push what you're typing to the desktop — without switching back and
forth to the main UniClipboard app.

<Callout type="warn">
  The keyboard **must have "Allow Full Access" enabled**, otherwise it can't read the system
  clipboard or reach your server, and is just an empty shell. This is a hard iOS restriction on
  third-party keyboards, unrelated to this app.
</Callout>

### Enable the Keyboard

<Steps>

<Step>

### Open the keyboard list in system Settings

Go to **Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards** on your iPhone. This lists all
currently enabled input methods.

</Step>

<Step>

### Add UniClip Keyboard

Tap **Add New Keyboard…** and select **UniClip Keyboard** under "Third-Party Keyboards".

</Step>

<Step>

### Allow Full Access

Back in the keyboard list, tap into the **UniClip Keyboard** you just added and turn on
**Allow Full Access**. The system will ask for confirmation once — choose Allow.
**Without this the keyboard won't work** (it can't read the clipboard or reach the server).

</Step>

<Step>

### Switch to it in any text field

In any app's text field, long-press the globe icon (🌐) at the bottom left and pick
**UniClip Keyboard** to use it.

</Step>

</Steps>

### Keyboard Settings and Status

The app's **Settings → Keyboard** has an enablement guide and a status display, with
three states: **Not Added / Added / Ready**.

<Callout type="info">
  The status isn't real-time — it refreshes from a heartbeat reported when you **open the keyboard
  once**. If you just added the keyboard in system Settings and the app still shows the old status,
  switch UniClip Keyboard into any text field and use it briefly, then come back to the app and the
  status will be updated.
</Callout>

The same page also lets you toggle **key sounds** and **haptic feedback** to your liking.

## Share Extension

The share extension lets you send content to UniClipboard directly from other apps. It
**appears in the system share sheet automatically once the app is installed — there's no
separate toggle**, and you don't need to enable anything inside the app.

To use it: tap the system share button in any app, find **UniClipboard** in the row of
app icons in the share sheet, and tap it — the content lands in your clipboard history
and is pushed to the desktop. It supports sharing **text, links, images, and a single
file**.

If UniClipboard is ranked too far down the share sheet, iOS has no API for an app to
promote itself, so you have to set it once by hand:

<Steps>

<Step>

### Open the share sheet and enter edit mode

Tap the share button in any app, scroll the row of app icons all the way to the right,
and tap **More** (the ellipsis icon).

</Step>

<Step>

### Set UniClipboard as a favorite

Find UniClipboard in the list that appears and tap the **green plus** on its left or
**Favorite** on its right. It will be pinned to an earlier position in the share sheet
so you can reach it at a glance from then on.

</Step>

</Steps>

## About Sharing Suggestions

<Callout type="info">
  UniClipboard donates some sharing suggestions to the "people / suggestions" strip at
  the **very top** of the iOS share sheet, so you can share content to it faster.

**These are not shortcuts in the Shortcuts app** — don't go looking for UniClipboard in
the Shortcuts app, it isn't there. These suggestions only appear in the top area of the
system share sheet.

</Callout>

## Main App and Extensions Share the Same Data

The main app, the keyboard extension, and the share extension all **share the same
clipboard history and settings** (via iOS App Group shared storage). So the content you
push from the keyboard, the content you send from the share sheet, and what you work with
in the main app all see the same history, and you only need to configure settings once in
the main app for all three to take effect.
